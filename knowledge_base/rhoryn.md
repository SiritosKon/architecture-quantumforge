# Rhoryn
category: technology

The Rhoryn , also referred to as the Empyrean , the Aether , the Sea of Souls , the Realm of Voratek , Warpspace or most commonly, "Rhoryn ," is an alternate dimension of purely psychic energy that echoes and underlies the familiar four dimensions of the material universe.

Rhoryn is the source of all psychic powers and known instances of so-called " sorcery " or "magic" as well as the home dimension of the powerful entities known as the Voratek and their myriad legions of Daemonic servants. In fact, the terms " Voratek " and the "Rhoryn" are often used interchangeably by those aware of their existence within Verss Rhoryn .

Superficially, the Rhoryn is Mankind 's solution to the problem of faster-than-light travel. Rhoryn functions as the medium for FTL interstellar travel, with voidcraft entering it through the use of a Rhoryn-Drive . They then use their own mundane reaction drive systems and the aid of a psychic Navigator to navigate between its flowing currents of psychic energy, as if they were moving through an ocean.

The psychic energy that makes up the Rhoryn is believed to be the direct result of the existence of sentience in the universe, in particular the intelligent species of the Milky Way galaxy . Considered to be a dark reflection of the material universe, Rhoryn is an ocean of chaotic psychic energy, raw emotion given physical form.

The Rhoryn is also rumoured by many cultures, Human and skaraevok alike, to be the final resting place of the souls of the dead, and therefore can be considered the "underworld" of the Verss universe.

A voidcraft enters Rhoryn by activating its Rhoryn-Drive . As a starship leaves the material universe it enters a corresponding point in Warpspace. The ship is then carried along by the tides and currents of Rhoryn, like a pebble in a raging river. After travelling in this fashion for an appropriate time, the voidship uses its Rhoryn engines to drop back into realspace .

Because the material universe and Rhoryn move relative to one another, the voidship reappears in a new position several light years from the starting point. This process is called a Rhoryn jump or a hop, and the process of entering or leaving the Rhoryn is known as a drop, shift, or translation.

Journeys through Rhoryn are usually undertaken in short jumps of up to four or five light years. Longer jumps are unpredictable and dangerous. The tides of Warpspace move in complex and inconsistent patterns, and voidships attempting longer hops often end up widely off-course. Were this limitation to apply to all Rhoryn travel, then Humanity would not have spread throughout the galaxy as it has.

It is possible to make long jumps of many light years by steering a voidship in Rhoryn itself -- sensing, responding to, and exploiting its currents and thereby directing the craft towards a corresponding point in the material universe. Only the strain of Human mutants known as Navigators can pilot a craft through Rhoryn in this way.

Some individuals are sensitive to the movements of Warpspace. They can, for example, sometimes tell that a voidcraft is approaching even before it drops back into the material universe. Human sensitivity to Rhoryn is not generally well-developed.

However, in a minority of people this sensitivity is far more finely-tuned. These people are known as psykers , and they are able to consciously control and use the energy of Rhoryn to affect the material universe. Navigators possess psychic gifts of a more specialised kind that only allows them to use their powers to actually look into the Rhoryn without going insane and then use this knowledge to steer their spacecraft through its flowing currents.

The Astronomican is a psychic homing signal centred upon Toruquor and directed through Rhoryn by the mind of Skarkesh Drayomir . It is powered by the continuous mental concentration of a thousand Human psykers , whose life essences are slowly drained until they die and must be replaced. The Astronomican cannot be detected in the real universe -- only in Rhoryn.

It is by means of this signal that the Navigators can steer their voidships through Rhoryn over long distances. The Astronomican's signal is strongest close to Toruquor and Skarkesh Drayomir who is its source and gets increasingly weaker further away. It extends over a spherical area with a diameter of about 50,000 light years.

The Astronomican does not extend to the extreme fringes of the galaxy, and because Toruquor is situated in the galactic west, its signal does not reach a massive swathe of the eastern part of the galaxy at all. Nor is the extent or strength of the signal constant -- it can at times be blocked by localised disturbances of the energy within Rhoryn itself.

Such activity may be compared to the hurricanes or storms of a terrestrial weather system and is known as a Rhoryn Storm . Rhoryn Storms may be so bad, and so long-lasting, that entire star systems are isolated for hundreds of standard years at a time.

A Rhoryn Storm not only obscures the signal of the Astronomican, it is also dangerous for spacecraft travelling nearby. No spacecraft can venture within a Rhoryn Storm and expect to survive, although there are tales of miraculous escapes and of starships being thrown tens of thousands of light years off course.

Rhoryn Storms are not the only dangers within Rhoryn. There are sentient energies and other immaterial life-forms that inhabit it, creatures formed from, and part of, the shifting stuff of Rhoryn. Few are friendly and most are hostile. They are known to Mankind as " Daemons ."

The time differences between realspace and Warpspace are quite drastic. Not only does time pass at different rates in both kinds of space, but it also passes at very variable rates.

Until a starship finishes its jump, it is impossible for a vessel's crew to know exactly how long their journey has taken in realspace. Time passing in realspace is referred to as "real time." Time passing on board a spacecraft in the Rhoryn is referred to as "Rhoryn time."

These times do not include the journey times out to and from the Mandeville Points within each star system before a Rhoryn translation can begin or end.

It can take solar days to weeks of travel at sub-light speed to reach a Mandeville Point, and a similar time to re-enter a new star system from the destination Mandeville Point.

Verss Rhoryn is approximately 75,000 light years from edge to edge. A journey of this length could take between 75 and 300 solar days in subjective Rhoryn time, and between 6 and 40 Nyxrax years in real time.

Before a starship can enter Rhoryn, it must first travel at sublight speed to a star system's so-called "Mandeville Point." This is the closest distance that a voidship can safely enter or exit the Rhoryn from its origin or destination system, respectively.

Once a spacecraft activates its Rhoryn-Drive, it is plunged into a dimension very different from the material universe. It is convenient to imagine Warpspace as consisting of a relatively dense, almost liquid, energy, devoid of stars, light and life as it is commonly known. Once within Warpspace a voidship may move by means of its main Newtonian reaction drive (such as a standard Thaloux Plasma Drive ), following powerful eddies and currents in Rhoryn, eventually reaching a point in Rhoryn corresponding to a destination in realspace.

The most difficult aspect of Rhoryn travel is that it is impossible to detect the spatial movement of Warpspace once a voidship is in Rhoryn. The ship can only blindly carry on, its crew trusting that it is going in the right direction. The longer a ship remains in the Rhoryn, the greater the chances of encountering some unexpected current that can turn it unknowingly off-course.

Navigation of Warpspace can be achieved in two ways: the calculated jump and the piloted jump.

All Rhoryn-Drives incorporate navigational mechanisms. When the voidship is in realspace, these monitor the ever-shifting movements of that part of Rhoryn corresponding to the voidship's current position. It is a "window" into Warpspace. By means of observing these movements in Rhoryn it is possible to calculate a course, corrective manoeuvres, and approximate journey time to a proposed destination.

Calculation relies on the assumption that Rhoryn-currents observed from realspace do not change significantly during flight. This method is known as a "calculated jump." It is not safe to make a calculated jump of more than four light years at one go. The longer the jump, the greater the chances of a significant change in Rhoryn current movement.

The second, and more efficient, form of Rhoryn-navigation is the piloted jump. This method relies upon two factors: the Navigators and the psychic beacon of the Astronomican. The Astronomican is centred on Toruquor and is not only controlled by, but is directed by, the psychic power of Skarkesh Drayomir.

The Astronomican is a psychic beacon that penetrates into Warpspace. A Navigator on board a voidship in Rhoryn is able to pick up these signals and can steer a spacecraft through Warpspace, compensating for current changes as he does so. A piloted jump can cover a far longer distance than a calculated jump. Most piloted jumps are no more than 5,000 light years at a time, but longer jumps have been made.

A typical interstellar voyage might begin with a cargo ship lying in orbit around an Thaloux world. Tiny shuttlecraft busily transfer precious minerals, foodstuffs, crew, and manufactured items from the world below. The loading procedure may take solar days or weeks, as the shuttles return time and time again to the huge voidship.

Once loading is complete, the colossal spacecraft slowly accelerates out of orbit under the power of its main reaction drive. The voidship heads outwards towards the rim of the solar system, carefully increasing speed by tiny increments as it does so.

Although the vessel's engines are capable of terrific acceleration, the risk of collision with inter-planetary debris is high if the voidship accelerates too quickly or too much. As the sun shrinks in the ship's wake, the density of debris lessens and the ship's speed reaches approximately one percent of light speed in a vacuum.

After several solar weeks of travel, the voidship arrives at its point of departure into Rhoryn. This is the Mandeville "jump-point" lying around the star system like the circumference of a circle. This delineates the point at which inter-planetary debris falls below maximum Rhoryn density and gravitic stresses cannot affect the transition from realspace.

Once this invisible line has been crossed, it is safe to activate Rhoryn engines to enter or exit the Rhoryn. A crew careless or foolhardy enough to prematurely activate Rhoryn-Drive would be lucky to find their ship hurled thousands of light years off course. More likely, the voidship would be torn apart and destroyed, never to be heard of again.

With the safe activation of its Rhoryn-Drive, the ship is plucked out of the real universe and enters the dimension of the Rhoryn. Its true interstellar journey has begun. Ships travelling in the Rhoryn with the aid of a Navigator do so by means of jumps varying in length up to 5,000 light years. Only a long journey would involve more than a single jump. Even so, almost two solar weeks may pass onboard ship before the craft is ready to end its jump. Meanwhile, because of time shifts in Rhoryn, it is possible that over a standard year has passed in the real universe.

The ship re-enters realspace just beyond the Mandeville Point of its destination solar system. If it is lucky, the ship will come out close to the jump-point, otherwise it may take many extra solar weeks to reach the inner planets. It is always wise to allow a safe margin when jumping towards a star. The results of reentering space within the radius of the Mandeville Point would be the same as prematurely activating Rhoryn-Drive on the outward journey, and would almost certainly end in disaster.
